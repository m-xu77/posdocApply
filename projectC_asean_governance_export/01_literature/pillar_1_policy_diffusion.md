# Pillar 1 — Policy Diffusion, Frame Travel, Ideational Transfer

**Role in project**: theoretical mechanism for why and how domestic policy frames migrate to outward discourse. Supplies Layer 1 and Layer 2 of the theoretical chain in `00_design/02_theoretical_framework.md`.

---

## Core references

### Snow, D.A. & Benford, R.D. (2000). "Framing processes and social movements." *Annual Review of Sociology* 26: 611–639.

- **Argument**: frames have diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational components.
- **Use in project**: the dictionary construction in Stage 1 treats each (actor × action × entry_mechanism) cell as a candidate frame and forces the LLM to articulate diagnostic + prognostic content rather than just collecting keywords.

### Béland, D. & Cox, R.H. (2013). "The politics of policy paradigms." *Governance* 26 (2): 193–195.

- **Argument**: ideas do political work — they mobilize, legitimate, and anchor institutions.
- **Use in project**: motivates the bureaucratic-divergence analysis (H2/H3). Different agencies are doing different political work with the frames they choose.

### Weyland, K. (2009). *Bounded Rationality and Policy Diffusion: Social Sector Reform in Latin America*. Princeton UP.

- **Argument**: distinguishes coercion / competition / learning / emulation mechanisms.
- **Use**: H5 (alignment vs. dependency) is partly a test of which mechanism best explains country variation.

### Sharman, J.C. (2008). "Power and discourse in policy diffusion: anti-money laundering in developing states." *International Studies Quarterly* 52 (3): 635–656.

- **Argument**: symbolic emulation — recipients adopt the *language* without the *practice*.
- **Use**: the symmetric question for *senders* — does China export language without practice? Provides the theoretical hook for measuring frames specifically rather than projects.

### Dolowitz, D. & Marsh, D. (2000). "Learning from abroad: the role of policy transfer in contemporary policy-making." *Governance* 13 (1): 5–23.

- **Argument**: typology of policy-transfer voluntariness and content.
- **Use**: background only; the project does not invoke voluntariness.

### Stone, D. (2008). "Global public policy, transnational policy communities, and their networks." *Policy Studies Journal* 36 (1): 19–38.

- **Argument**: policy travels through transnational communities and transfer agents.
- **Use**: motivates including GDI Friends Group materials as a "transfer-agent" channel distinct from MFA broadcast.

### Solingen, E. (2012). "Of dominoes and firewalls: the international politics of diffusion." *International Studies Quarterly* 56 (4): 631–644.

- **Argument**: emphasizes domestic coalitions in receiving states as filters.
- **Use**: footnote — our project is sender-side and explicitly leaves uptake to future work.

### Béland, D. (2009). "Ideas, institutions, and policy change." *Journal of European Public Policy* 16 (5): 701–718.

- **Argument**: institutions are sticky carriers of ideas.
- **Use**: motivates H6 — the 2018 CIDCA founding (institutional creation) is hypothesized to be a stronger break than 2021 GDI (rebranding).
