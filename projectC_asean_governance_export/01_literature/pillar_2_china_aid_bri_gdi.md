# Pillar 2 — China's Foreign Aid, BRI, GDI, South-South Cooperation

**Role in project**: empirical landscape. Tells us what is known about China's outward engagement and where this project sits in that scholarship.

---

## Core references

### Brautigam, D. (2009). *The Dragon's Gift: The Real Story of China in Africa*. OUP.

- **Argument**: China's African engagement is more commercial-pragmatic than ideological.
- **Use**: anchor for the "infrastructure-only" camp; the headline counter-claim we test.

### Brautigam, D. (2011). "Chinese development aid in Africa: what, where, why, and how much?" In Golley & Song (eds.), *China Update*.

- **Argument**: clarifies the China Development Bank vs. CIDCA-precursor vs. SOE blurring.
- **Use**: motivates the actor-disaggregated design (H2/H3).

### Hameiri, S. & Jones, L. (2018). "China challenges global governance? Chinese international development finance and the AIIB." *International Affairs* 94 (3): 573–593.

- **Argument**: AIIB and related institutions accommodate rather than challenge global governance norms.
- **Use**: a *frames* version of this paper is what the project produces.

### Halper, S. (2010). *The Beijing Consensus*. Basic Books.

- **Argument**: China actively offers an alternative governance model.
- **Use**: the "governance export" claim we test empirically against the discourse.

### Dreher, A., Fuchs, A., Parks, B., Strange, A. & Tierney, M.J. (2021). *Banking on Beijing: The Aid and Credit of the Chinese Development Banks*. Cambridge UP.

- **Argument**: AidData project-level statistics on Chinese loans and grants.
- **Use**: source for country-year covariates (aid-per-capita, project count).

### Custer, S., Russell, B., DiLorenzo, M., Cheng, M., Ghose, S., Sims, J., Turner, J. & Desai, H. (2018). *Ties That Bind: Quantifying China's Public Diplomacy and Its "Good Neighbor" Effect*. AidData.

- **Argument**: measures Chinese public diplomacy reach.
- **Use**: comparison benchmark for the country-variation analysis.

### Jones, L. & Hameiri, S. (2020). "Debunking the myth of 'debt-trap diplomacy.'" Chatham House.

- **Argument**: debunks a widely cited but empirically thin narrative.
- **Use**: methodological cautionary tale — narrative-driven analyses generate misleading claims; the discipline of countable frames is the antidote.

### Bräutigam, D. & Hwang, J. (2019). "Great walls over African rivers." *Development Policy Review* 37 (2): 247–260.

- **Argument**: clarifies financing terms.
- **Use**: background.

### Hong, J. (2024). "CIDCA at five: bureaucratic consolidation in China's foreign aid." *Journal of Contemporary China* (forthcoming/recent — verify cite).

- **Argument**: CIDCA's organizational footprint and consolidation post-2018.
- **Use**: empirical anchor for H6 (2018 break) and H3 (CIDCA outlier).

### Liu, T. & Tan, K. (2023). "Bureaucratic competition and China's outward development discourse." *China Quarterly* (verify).

- **Argument**: documents MFA / MOFCOM / CIDCA jurisdictional friction.
- **Use**: H2/H3 direct empirical antecedent — this project provides the frame-level resolution they lack.

## Where this project sits

This project is **complementary** to AidData (which counts projects and money) and **directly extends** Liu & Tan / Hong (which look at agency politics qualitatively). It introduces measurable frame-level structure into a literature dominated by either large-N project analysis or small-n discourse analysis.
